## 0:00-0:20 - Slide 1 - Introduction

Hello, we are Ctrl+S. Our project is Aiversecity, a career exploration prototype for the Aiversecity Future-Ready Workforce domain. We focus on one decision a university student or recent graduate faces: which role should I explore, and what should I practise first?

## 0:20-0:55 - Slide 2 - Problem

Public AI news, occupation descriptions and course pages often answer different questions. A learner still needs to decide whether a source applies to their location, whether it describes a changing task or a disappearing job, and how it connects to their own next learning step. The ILO's 2025 study estimates that one in four workers globally is in an occupation with some generative AI exposure. That is global task-exposure context. We do not interpret it as a Malaysian job-loss rate. We have not yet validated our user assumptions through interviews.

## 0:55-1:15 - Slide 3 - The journey

Our fictional learner records skills they have tried and answers four workplace questions. The app compares three supported role families, explains its score and skill gaps, and builds an ordered practice pathway. Each role and skill has an SDG explanation. The learner can compare two explicit AI-adoption assumptions and inspect evidence freshness.

## 1:15-2:05 - Slide 4 then browser - Intake and matching

Let us use a fictional test profile. These are demonstration answers, not a real CV. I select a context, mark skills tried, and answer the workplace questions. The app evaluates three response dimensions with a documented deterministic rubric. Here are the role scores and their weighted components. This score is exploration alignment. It is not a probability of employment or a validated assessment of competence. The skills I selected remain self-reported. They influence the learning pathway rather than adding score points. Only three role families are supported, so the shortlist is deliberately limited.

## 2:05-3:05 - Browser - Trend evidence and changed output

The personal score and the trend interpretation are separate. This source is a U.S. BLS long-range projection for Data Scientists, used as a related-occupation proxy for Data Analyst. The interface shows its publication date, retrieval time and low confidence for transferring it to a Malaysian career choice. Eligible positive evidence moves the related role earlier for exploration. Negative evidence would move it later. Other roles have unknown direction from this source.

When evidence is missing, stale or fails the documented freshness checks, the trend adjustment clears. We do not use headline counts as a disruption index. UN and MOSTI headlines offer context and do not establish hiring demand.

## 3:05-3:50 - Slide 5 then browser - Learning and uncertainty

I choose a role to inspect its Foundation, Specialization and Capstone pathway. Each step names a skill, prerequisite, practice task and public learning resource. The time is our practice budget, not the provider's full course duration or a promise of job readiness. Editing the self-reported skills changes the order only when prerequisites allow. Under the second planning assumption, verification and explanation receive more emphasis. This is a qualitative scenario, not a forecast. Checking a task as complete records a statement in this session. It does not verify mastery.

[Show the skill editor, scenario change, preserved prerequisite and a resource link.]

## 3:50-4:20 - Slide 6 - Proposed SDG contribution

The primary target is UN Target 4.4, relevant technical and vocational skills for employment. We connect each skill to a concrete practice task. Roles also have conditional links to productivity under Target 8.2 or technological capability under Target 9.5. These are proposed contributions. We have not measured learning gains, employment outcomes or SDG indicator changes.

## 4:20-4:55 - Slide 7 - Architecture and responsible use

React and TypeScript run the session interface. Fixed server endpoints retrieve public BLS and official RSS evidence, with a thirty-minute cache and dated failure states. The assessment answers remain in browser memory and clear on reload. There is no application database, runtime language model or GPU workload. Our core matching and priority rules are deterministic. Codex provided major assistance with development and materials. Registered team members must review, test and understand that work before signing the submission.

## 4:55-5:30 - Slide 8 - Evaluation plan

Our next evaluation uses fictional profiles and a timed comparison against browsing the same sources. Proposed Phase 2 targets are a twenty percent reduction in median decision-task time and at least an eighty percent pass rate on the comprehension check. These are targets. We have no measured baseline yet. We will report the paired sample size, actual task times and every failure. The comprehension check asks what the score means, which geography the evidence covers and which prerequisite comes first.

## 5:30-6:00 - Slide 9 - Limits and release status

The rubric and scenario weights need validation. U.S. projections cannot establish Malaysian hiring demand, and a public news feed can contain old or irrelevant stories. We expose those limits and preserve user choice.
